Kronosaurus queenslandicus was a huge marine reptile that lived in the seas around Australia during the Early Cretaceous, around 125 to 100 million years ago. It belonged to the pliosaur group and had a massive head, a short neck and four powerful flippers. Its body was adapted for life in the water.

Fossils of Kronosaurus were discovered in Queensland, Australia, in rocks that were formed in an ancient inland sea. This marine environment contained many different animals, including fish, turtles, ammonites and other marine reptiles. Kronosaurus was one of the largest predators in this ecosystem.

Its enormous jaws were armed with large, conical teeth that could grip and tear prey. Kronosaurus probably hunted fish, turtles and other marine reptiles, using its powerful flippers to accelerate through the water. Its large head gave it an especially strong bite compared with many other marine reptiles.

Kronosaurus was named after Cronus, a powerful figure from Greek mythology. It was one of the most formidable marine predators of the Early Cretaceous seas and shows how large and specialized marine reptiles could become during the age of dinosaurs.
