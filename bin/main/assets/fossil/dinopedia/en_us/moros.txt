Moros intrepidus was a small tyrannosauroid dinosaur that lived in what is now Utah, USA, during the Late Cretaceous, around 96 million years ago. It lived millions of years before the appearance of Tyrannosaurus rex and was much smaller than the giant tyrannosaurs that later became dominant predators. Moros was about three metres long and had a lightweight body.

Fossils of Moros were discovered in the Cedar Mountain Formation of Utah. At the time, the region contained rivers, forests and floodplains that supported many different animals. Moros lived alongside much larger dinosaurs and other predators, meaning that it probably had to rely on speed and agility rather than size.

Moros had long, slender hind limbs that suggest it was a relatively fast and agile animal. Its body was adapted for moving quickly while hunting smaller prey or avoiding larger predators. As a tyrannosauroid, it was part of the evolutionary lineage that eventually produced some of the largest land predators in Earth's history.

The name Moros means "doom" or "impending fate" in Greek mythology, while the species name intrepidus means "fearless". Moros is important because it shows that small tyrannosauroids were still present in North America shortly before giant tyrannosaurids became the dominant predators.
