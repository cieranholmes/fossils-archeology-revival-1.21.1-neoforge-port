Eolambia caroljonesa was a herbivorous dinosaur that lived in North America during the Early Cretaceous, around 100 million years ago. It belonged to the hadrosauroid group, which was closely related to the later duck-billed dinosaurs. Eolambia had a long body, a strong tail and powerful hind limbs that allowed it to move efficiently across its environment.

Fossils of Eolambia were discovered in Utah, USA, in the Cedar Mountain Formation. The area contained rivers, floodplains and large areas of vegetation that provided food for many herbivorous dinosaurs. Eolambia shared its environment with several other dinosaurs, as well as predators that hunted the smaller and weaker members of the ecosystem.

Eolambia had a beak-like mouth that was useful for cropping plants. It probably fed on leaves, shoots and other vegetation growing close to the ground. Its strong hind limbs and long tail helped it maintain balance while moving, and it may have been capable of running quickly when threatened by predators.

The name Eolambia means "dawn of the lambeosaur", referring to its early position in the evolution of the hadrosaur lineage. Eolambia is an important dinosaur for understanding how the first hadrosauroids developed before the appearance of the more specialized duck-billed dinosaurs.
