Sauroposeidon proteles was a gigantic sauropod dinosaur that lived in North America during the Early Cretaceous, around 110 million years ago. It belonged to the brachiosaurid group, which included long-necked herbivores with tall bodies and powerful limbs. Sauroposeidon had an exceptionally long neck that allowed it to reach vegetation high above the ground.

Fossils of Sauroposeidon were discovered in Oklahoma, USA, in rocks that preserve evidence of ancient floodplains and river environments. The region supported a wide variety of plants and animals. Sauroposeidon would have shared its habitat with other dinosaurs, including smaller herbivores and predators.

Its enormous size would have made Sauroposeidon difficult for most predators to attack when fully grown. Its long neck allowed it to feed from trees and other tall vegetation while using its large body to support its weight. Like other sauropods, it probably spent much of its time feeding to obtain enough energy to support its huge body.

Sauroposeidon was one of the tallest dinosaurs known, although its exact size is difficult to determine because its fossil remains are incomplete. Its name refers to a gigantic "lizard" associated with Poseidon, the ancient Greek god of the sea and earthquakes. The discovery of Sauroposeidon shows how enormous some sauropods could become.
