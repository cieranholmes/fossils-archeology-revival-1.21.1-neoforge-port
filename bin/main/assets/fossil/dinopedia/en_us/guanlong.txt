Guanlong wucaii was an early tyrannosauroid dinosaur that lived in what is now China during the Late Jurassic, around 160 million years ago. It lived roughly 90 million years before Tyrannosaurus rex and was much smaller than the giant tyrannosaurs that appeared later. Guanlong was about four metres long and had a lightweight body.

Fossils of Guanlong were discovered in the Shishugou Formation of Xinjiang, China. The region contained forests, rivers and floodplains that supported many different prehistoric animals. Guanlong lived alongside other dinosaurs and predators, probably hunting smaller animals while avoiding larger threats.

One of its most distinctive features was a large crest running along the top of its skull. The crest was relatively thin and fragile, so it may have been used mainly for display rather than combat. Guanlong also had a long tail and strong hind limbs, which probably helped it move quickly through its environment.

The name Guanlong means "crown dragon", referring to the unusual crest on its head. Guanlong is important because it shows that the tyrannosauroid lineage already included small and medium-sized predators long before the evolution of the giant tyrannosaurs.
