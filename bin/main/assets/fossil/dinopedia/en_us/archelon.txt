Archelon ischyros was the largest known sea turtle and lived in the warm Cretaceous seas of North America around 80 million years ago. It had a broad body, powerful front flippers and a flexible shell structure that differed from the hard, fully enclosed shells of modern sea turtles. Its large body made it one of the most impressive marine reptiles of its time.

Fossils of Archelon have been discovered in South Dakota and other parts of the central United States. During the Late Cretaceous, much of this region was covered by a large inland sea known as the Western Interior Seaway. The sea was home to many fish, marine reptiles, sharks and other animals that shared the same environment.

Archelon could reach more than four metres in length and had enormous front flippers that allowed it to travel through the water. Its flexible shell may have helped reduce its weight while swimming. It probably spent much of its life in the open sea and fed on soft-bodied animals and other available marine food.

Like modern sea turtles, Archelon was adapted to a life spent mostly in the water. Its powerful flippers and streamlined body allowed it to travel long distances through the ancient seas. Its fossils provide an important look at the enormous diversity of marine life that existed during the Late Cretaceous.
