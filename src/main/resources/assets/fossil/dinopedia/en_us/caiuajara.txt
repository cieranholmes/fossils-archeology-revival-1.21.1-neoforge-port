Caiuajara dobruskii was a small to medium-sized pterosaur that lived in Brazil during the Late Cretaceous. It belonged to the tapejarid group and had a short face, toothless jaws and a prominent crest on its head. Its wings allowed it to fly efficiently, while its strong limbs also helped it move across the ground.

Fossils of Caiuajara were discovered in the Goio-Erê Formation of southern Brazil. Large numbers of specimens were found together at the same site, including individuals of different ages. This discovery provides evidence that Caiuajara may have lived in groups and possibly gathered together around lakes, rivers or other sources of water.

Caiuajara had a relatively short skull with a large crest that became more developed as the animal grew. The crest may have been used for display or communication between members of the species. Like other tapejarids, it probably used its beak to feed on a variety of food available in its environment.

The discovery of many Caiuajara fossils makes this pterosaur especially useful for studying the growth and behavior of flying reptiles. Its fossils show that pterosaurs were not always solitary animals and that some species may have formed large groups during parts of their lives.
