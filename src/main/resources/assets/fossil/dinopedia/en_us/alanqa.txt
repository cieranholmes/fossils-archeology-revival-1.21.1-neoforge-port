Alanqa saharica was a large pterosaur that lived in North Africa during the Late Cretaceous, around 100 million years ago. It belonged to the azhdarchoid group, a clade of long-necked pterosaurs that included some of the largest flying reptiles ever known. Alanqa had a long, narrow beak, a long neck and large wings that allowed it to travel through the air in search of food.

Fossils of Alanqa were discovered in the Kem Kem beds of Morocco, a region that once contained rivers, floodplains and wetlands. The environment was home to many prehistoric animals, including fish, crocodile-like reptiles, dinosaurs and other pterosaurs. Alanqa shared this ecosystem with several large predators and flying reptiles.

Recently reclassified into its own different family Alanqidae. Alanqa and it's relatives may have had a more mollusk heavy diet compared to its Azhdarchid relatives by probing sandy beaches similar to sandpipers today based on the fossils of Alanqa's relative Argentinadraco.
