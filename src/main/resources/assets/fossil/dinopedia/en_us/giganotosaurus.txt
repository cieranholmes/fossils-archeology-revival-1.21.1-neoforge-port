Giganotosaurus carolinii was one of the largest known terrestrial predatory dinosaurs. It lived in Patagonia, Argentina, during the Late Cretaceous, around 99 million years ago. Giganotosaurus belonged to the carcharodontosaurid group and had a massive body, powerful hind limbs and a long skull filled with sharp, serrated teeth.

Fossils of Giganotosaurus were discovered in the Candeleros Formation of Argentina. The region contained rivers, floodplains and forests that supported a wide variety of dinosaurs. Giganotosaurus lived alongside enormous sauropods, including some of the largest herbivores known from South America.

Its long skull was filled with blade-like teeth that were well suited for cutting flesh. Giganotosaurus probably hunted large animals and may have attacked young or weakened sauropods. Its size made it one of the dominant predators in its ecosystem, although smaller prey would also have been an important source of food.

Giganotosaurus could reach around 12 to 13 metres in length, although estimates vary because the fossil remains are incomplete. Its name means "giant southern lizard", referring to its enormous size and the southern continent where it was discovered. It was one of the most formidable predators of Late Cretaceous South America.
