Aquilops americanus was a small ceratopsian dinosaur that lived in North America during the Early Cretaceous, around 109 million years ago. It belonged to an early group of horned dinosaurs that later included famous animals such as Triceratops. Aquilops was much smaller than its later relatives, reaching only around 60 centimeters in length. It had a small beak-like mouth that was well suited for feeding on plants.

Fossils of Aquilops were discovered in Montana, USA, in rocks belonging to the Cloverly Formation. The area was part of a warm environment with rivers, forests and open vegetation. Aquilops lived alongside many other dinosaurs and prehistoric animals, including small predators that may have posed a threat to such a small herbivore.

Aquilops had a lightweight skull with a pointed beak and a relatively small body. Its beak was probably useful for biting and cutting vegetation. Although it was primarily a plant-eater, the exact details of its diet are uncertain. Its small size may have allowed it to feed on plants that were difficult for larger herbivorous dinosaurs to reach.

The name Aquilops means "eagle face", referring to the shape of its skull and beak. It represents one of the earliest known ceratopsians from North America and provides important evidence about the early evolution of the horned dinosaur group.
