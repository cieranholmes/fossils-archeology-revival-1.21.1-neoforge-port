Microvenator celer was a small theropod dinosaur that lived in North America during the Early Cretaceous, around 110 million years ago. It belonged to the oviraptorosaur lineage and was much smaller than many of the better-known predatory dinosaurs. Microvenator had a lightweight body and was probably an active and agile animal.

Its fossils were discovered in Montana, USA, in rocks of the Cloverly Formation. The region contained forests, rivers and open areas inhabited by many different dinosaurs. Because of its small size, Microvenator would have lived alongside much larger predators and probably relied on speed and agility to survive.

Microvenator had long hind limbs and a relatively lightweight skeleton. It may have fed on small animals, insects, eggs and other available food. Its exact diet is uncertain because the known fossil material is incomplete, but its anatomy suggests that it was an active animal capable of moving quickly through its environment.

The name Microvenator means "small hunter", referring to its relatively small body size compared with other predatory dinosaurs. Although its fossils are incomplete, Microvenator provides an interesting example of the smaller theropods that lived alongside the larger predators of the Early Cretaceous.
